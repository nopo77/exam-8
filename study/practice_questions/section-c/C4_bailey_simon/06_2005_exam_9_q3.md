# 2005 Exam 9 - Q3

**Points:** 3

## Question

### Part a (2.00 pts)

Given the following information:

N = the number of drivers in the population m = the mean claim frequency of all drivers Mod = the credibility weighted modification factors for risks with one or more claims in the past year

Derive the formula for the credibility assigned to the experience of drivers with one or more claims in the past year.

Assume that claim frequency follows a Poisson distribution.

### Part b (1.00 pts)

If there is a switch from a less refined class plan to a highly refined class plan, describe the likely change in the credibility assigned to an individual risk.

## Solution

### Part a

Let X = # of claims

Pr (risk was accident free last year) = Pr(X = 0) = e^(-m) Pr (risk had at least one accident last year) = 1 - Pr(X = 0) = 1 - e^(-m)

# of risks accident free last year = N * Pr(X = 0) = N(e^(-m)) # of risks with at least one accident last year; N * Pr(X > 0) = N(1 - e^(-m))

expected # of claims last year = Nm freq = avg # of claims last year for current B risks = (Nm) / [N(1 - e^(-m))] = m/(1 - e^(-m))

R = (freq for B)/(overall frequency) = m/(1 - e^(-m))/m = 1/(1 - e^(-m))

Z = (M - 1) / (R - 1) = (M - 1) / ((1/(1 - e^(-m))) - 1 )

### Part b

Credibility for an individual risk is lowered when the class plan is highly refined, because it is more difficult to identify differences in the loss potential for the particular risk at-hand from the average risk in the class.
