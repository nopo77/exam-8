# 2005 Exam 9 - Q2

**Points:** 3

## Question

### Part a (1.50 pts)

Expected losses for a risk within a class are projected based on the formula:

E = ZX + (1-Z)P, where

X = the most recent accident year's losses P = the prior estimate of the most recent accident year Z = the credibility assigned to the most recent accident year

Assume:

- No delays in obtaining data

10% — Z

What is the difference in weight given to Accident Year 2001 losses in Accident Year 2002's estimate and the weight given to Accident Year 2001 losses in Accident Year 2005's estimate?

### Part b (1.50 pts)

If there are significant shifts in risk parameters that require Z to be reevaluated, will the answer to part (a) above increase, decrease, or remain constant? Explain your answer.

Assume that there are no changes other than the shifts in risk parameters.

## Solution

### Part a

Note that E for each year becomes the value of P for the following year.

E_02 = 0.1X_01 + 0.9P_01 E_03 = 0.1X_02 + 0.9[0.1X_01 + 0.9P_01] E_04 = 0.1X_03 + 0.9[0.1X_02 + 0.9[0.1X_01 + 0.9P_01]] E_05 = 0.1X_04 + … + (0.1*0.9^3)X_01

Weight given to X_01 in E_05 estimate:

The weight given to AY 2001 losses in the 2002 estimate is the coefficient of X_01 in the formula for E_02 , which is 10%. The weight given to AY 2001 losses in the 2005 is the coefficient of X_01 in the formula for E_05 .

Difference

### Part b

If there is a significant shift in risk parameters, then historical data will be less predictive of future data. As such, relatively more weight should be given to recent historical data and less weight should be given to older historical data. This would cause Z to increase. With Z being larger, the value of Z - (Z)(1 - Z)^3 will also increase.

Note that the function f(x) = x - x(1 - x)^3 is strictly increasing on the interval [0,1]. As such an increase in x results in a increase of f(x) . When thinking about what happens to Z, remember that with exponential smoothing, all the credibility will be given to historical data (i.e., there is no other complement of credibility). In other words, this question is similar to asking how you will weight each of the last 5 year loss ratios in a rate indication. The weight given to each historical year has to add up to 100%, so if older years are less relevant, then more weight should be given to the recent years.
