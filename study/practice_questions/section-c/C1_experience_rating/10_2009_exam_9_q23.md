# 2009 Exam 9 - Q23

**Points:** 2.5

## Question

The following information was taken from two different experience rating plans:

|   | Experience Mod. |   | Experience data |   |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Insured | Plan A | Plan B | Manual Premium ($) | Losses ($) |
| a | 0.62 | 0.66 | 100,000 | 65,000 |
| b | 0.92 | 0.96 | 100,000 | 95,000 |
| c | 1.00 | 0.97 | 100,000 | 97,000 |
| d | 1.06 | 1.06 | 100,000 | 107,000 |
| e | 1.14 | 1.07 | 100,000 | 110,000 |

### Part a (2.00 pts)

Demonstrate whether plan A or plan B appears to be more equitable.

### Part b (0.50 pts)

Demonstrate whether plan A or plan B gives too much credit for good experience.

## Solution

### Part a

First we can calculate the standard loss ratios as standard loss ratio = losses / (manual premium * mod). Next we can use either the Quintiles Test or Efficiency Test. Really, these shouldn't be used on individual risks, so it would also have been reasonable if you attempted to group the risks into 3 groups before performing the test. Note that both plans are already sorted by the mods in increasing order.

Solution 1: Quintiles Test

Standard LRs under plan B are less dispersed than plan A, so plan B is more equitable.

Solution 2: Efficiency Test

| B | C |
| --- | --- |
| Plan A Test Stat | 0.043 |
| Plan B Test Stat | 0.009 |

<details><summary>Formulas</summary>

- `C32` = `=VAR.S(I8:I12)/VAR.S(L8:L12)`
- `C33` = `=VAR.S(J8:J12)/VAR.S(L8:L12)`

</details>

Since Plan B's efficiency test stat is lower, plan B is more equitable.

### Part b

Plan A appears to give too much credit to good experience, since insureds with a credit mod have a higher standard loss ratio than insureds with a debit mod.
