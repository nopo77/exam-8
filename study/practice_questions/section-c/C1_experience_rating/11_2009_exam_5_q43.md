# 2009 Exam 5 - Q43

**Points:** 1

## Question

An insurance company uses experience rating and schedule rating to calculate Commercial General Liability (CGL) premium for bowling ball manufacturers.

- A schedule rating credit of up to 10% can be judgmentally given for loss control programs.
- There are no caps on the experience modification factors.
- The insured is a bowling ball manufacturer whose loss control program has reduced losses by

an estimated 5% each year for the last 10 years.

Determine the appropriate schedule rating credit, assuming no changes to the insured's loss control program. Briefly explain your answer.

## Solution

It isn't clear whether the loss reduction impact was one-time (i.e., 5% reduction in all years compared to the level without loss control) or ongoing (i.e., year 2 had a 5% reduction from year 1, year 3 had a 5% reduction from year 2, etc.). We also don't know how much credibility is given to this insured's experience. As such, I'll show several possible answers.

Solution 1: Assume experience is fully credible, and loss control impact was 1-time

No schedule credit would be warranted since the loss control program is fully reflected in the experience used to determine the experience modification factor. Giving a schedule credit would be double-counting the impact on the experience.

Solution 2: Assume experience is NOT fully credible, and loss control impact was 1-time

A partial schedule credit could be warranted if the insured's experience is not fully credible. Even though the loss control program would be reflected in the full experience period for experience rating, the insured's experience not getting full credibility would mean a partial credit could still be appropriate.

Solution 3: Assume experience is fully credible, and loss control impact is ongoing

Since the loss control program continues to improve each year, the experience period for experience rating would reflect older levels of improvement of the program, and not the current larger levels of loss reduction it is causing. As such, it would be appropriate to give a schedule credit based on the expected difference between the historical loss reduction of the program and the future loss reduction.
