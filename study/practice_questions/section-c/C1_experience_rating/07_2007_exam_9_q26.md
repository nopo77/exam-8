# 2007 Exam 9 - Q26

**Points:** 2

## Question

An insurer is reviewing its workers compensation experience rating plan. The table below summarizes the average loss ratios for small and large risks based on both manual and standard premium and separates these loss ratios into five groups based on the quintile into which the current experience modification factor lies.

Average Loss Ratio

|   | Manual Premium |   | Standard Premium |   |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Experience Modification Factor Quintile | Small Risks | Large Risks | Small Risks | Large Risks |
| Lowest | 62% | 71% | 83% | 77% |
| Second | 67% | 72% | 80% | 76% |
| Third | 72% | 75% | 71% | 77% |
| Fourth | 77% | 77% | 67% | 76% |
| Highest | 85% | 80% | 69% | 77% |

Based on this data, discuss the performance of this insurer's experience rating plan, for both small and large risks, with respect to each of the following criteria.

### Part a (1.00 pts)

How well the plan identifies differences between risks.

### Part b (1.00 pts)

How appropriately the plan recognizes the actual experience of each risk.

## Solution

Note: while the question wording refers to "the insurer's" workers compensation experience rating plan, experience rating for Work Comp is done at the industry level, not the insurer level. Regardless, that wouldn't impact how you answer the question.

### Part a

The plan seems to identify differences between risks on both small and large risks to some degree since the manual loss ratios are increasing with the mods. There is more dispersion in the manual loss ratios for small risks, so the plan does better at identifying differences for small risks relative to large risks.

### Part b

The plan seems to apply a good amount of credibility for large risks as the standard loss ratios show no trend and are nearly equal across quintiles. The plan seems to apply too much credibility for small risks since the standard loss ratios show a decreasing trend.
