# 2004 Exam 9 - Q39 revised

**Points:** 3

## Question

An actuary is evaluating four different experience rating plans using the information in the table below. Each insured has premium greater than $100,000.

Loss Ratios to Standard Premium

| Insured with Experience Mods between | Plan A | Plan B | Plan C | Plan D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.800 - 0.899 | 0.985 | 1.020 | 0.999 | 0.995 |
| 0.900 - 0.999 | 0.989 | 1.015 | 1.005 | 0.999 |
| 1.000 - 1.099 | 1.022 | 1.000 | 1.012 | 1.000 |
| 1.100 - 1.199 | 1.015 | 0.985 | 0.995 | 1.005 |
| 1.200 - 1.299 | 1.025 | 0.980 | 1.015 | 1.015 |

### Part a (0.75 pts)

Which is the best performing plan and why?

### Part b (2.25 pts)

Explain why each of the other three plans is inferior to the best performing plan.

## Solution

The setup of this problem is flawed, since each plan would have its own mods, and we would want to sort risks and create quintiles separately for each plan. For example, a risk with a mod of 0.85 in plan A might have a mod of 0.93 in plan B, but the way the problem is presented doesn't allow for that possibility. Despite that major flaw in the question, I'll pretend that isn't an issue and solve accordingly.

We can use either the Quintiles Test or look at the variance in standard loss ratios.

### Part a

Solution 1: Quintiles Test

Plan C is the best performing plan since the standard loss ratios show no trend and they are close to unity.

Solution 2: Calculate variance in standard loss ratios.

Variances — 0.000355 — `=VAR.S(C9:C13)` — 0.000312 — `=VAR.S(D9:D13)` — 0.000071 — `=VAR.S(E9:E13)` — 0.000059 — `=VAR.S(F9:F13)`

Plan D is the best performing plan since it has the lowest variance in standard loss ratios.

### Part b

Solution 1: Quintiles Test

Plan A shows an overall increasing trend in standard loss ratios with the mods. This could mean the plan does not give enough credibility to loss experience.

Plan B shows a clear decreasing trend in standard loss ratios with the mods, indicating the credibility in the plan is too high.

Plan D shows a clear increasing trend in standard loss ratios with the mods, indicating the credibility in the plan is too low.

Solution 2: Calculate variance in standard loss ratios.

Plans A, B, and C all have more variance in the standard loss ratios as calculated in part (a) above.
