# Practice Problem 8 - Fisher Chapter 1 Q6

## Question

Suppose you are pricing a risk (which will be experience rated). This particular risk has a significantly better safety program than most other risks. Would it be appropriate to apply a schedule credit to reflect lower than average loss potential due to this superior safety program?

## Solution

Probably not. If the safety program in question does in fact reduce this risk's loss potential, this will be reflected in the risk's past experience and will be picked up by the experience rating. Using a schedule credit would double-count the expected benefit of the safety program. However, if the safety program is new (i.e., it was implemented during or after the experience period used by the experience rating program) then there is some expected benefit that would not be reflected in the past experience.

On the other hand, if the risk's experience isn't fully credible, then some partial schedule credit could be appropriate.
