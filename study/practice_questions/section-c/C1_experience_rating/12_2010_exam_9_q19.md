# 2010 Exam 9 - Q19

**Points:** 1.75

## Question

Respond to the following with respect to the NCCI experience rating formula:

### Part a (0.50 pts)

Explain why splitting the formula into primary and excess components is preferable for workers compensation risks.

### Part b (0.25 pts)

Briefly describe the function of the primary component of the formula.

### Part c (0.25 pts)

Briefly describe why the excess component is capped.

### Part d (0.75 pts)

Discuss how using full credibility conflicts with one goal of experience rating.

## Solution

### Part a

Solution 1: Discuss splitting process and parameter risk

Splitting losses into primary and excess components results in more accurate estimates of expected losses, because this would separate the parameter uncertainty related to the volume of claims in the primary layer, and the process risk related to the severity volatility in the excess layer. This works well for Work Comp since it has many small Medical-Only claims, and fewer large and uncertain Lost-Time claims.

Solution 2: Discuss how a split plan is a good compromise instead of non-linear transformations

Split plans are preferable for heavy-tailed loss distributions like Work Comp, for which the traditional credibility-weighting approach of no-split plans will often be inaccurate. While it might be ideal to use a non-linear transformation of the data (e.g., using the logarithm of losses), that would result in complex equations, and split plans are a good compromise.

### Part b

The primary component models the frequency of the loss distribution.

### Part c

The capping prevents large losses that are not predictive of future losses from having too big of an impact on the insured's premium.

### Part d

Giving full credibility to loss experience conflicts with the goal of individual risk equity, which is to charge insureds for their true loss potential. Giving full credibility to an insured's loss experience would penalize the insured for having random losses that do not reflect their true expected loss.
