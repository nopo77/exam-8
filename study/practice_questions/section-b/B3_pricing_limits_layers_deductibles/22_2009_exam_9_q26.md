# 2009 Exam 9 - Q26

**Points:** 2.5

## Question

The following claim count distribution by size of loss is available for a small book of business:

| B | C | D | E | F |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Size of Loss | $5 | $10 | $15 | $25 |
| Claim Count | 50 | 20 | 20 | 10 |

### Part a (1.00 pts)

Calculate the loss elimination ratio for a straight $5 deductible.

### Part b (1.50 pts)

The insurer is considering a disappearing deductible where the $5 deductible would disappear at $20. Calculate the resulting loss elimination ratio.

Size of Loss — $5 — $10 — $15 — $25

## Solution

### Part a

| B | D |
| --- | --- |
| Loss Eliminated | 500 |
| GU Loss | 1,000 |

<details><summary>Formulas</summary>

- `D17` = `=5*SUM(C7:F7)`
- `D18` = `=SUMPRODUCT(C6:F6,C7:F7)`

</details>

**LER:** 50% — `=D17/D18`

### Part b

Ded — $5 — $3.33 — `=5/(20-5)*(20-D22)` — $1.67 — `=5/(20-5)*(20-E22)` — $0

**Loss Eliminated:** 350 — `=SUMPRODUCT(C23:F23,C7:F7)`

**LER:** 35% — `=C25/D18`

You could have instead used the FORECAST function to get the deductibles between d and D:

| Claim size | Effective Deductible |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | 5 |
| 20 | 0 |

| B | C |
| --- | --- |
| $10 | 3.333333 |
| $15 | 1.666667 |

<details><summary>Formulas</summary>

- `C35` = `=FORECAST(B35,$C$32:$C$33,$B$32:$B$33)`
- `C36` = `=FORECAST(B36,$C$32:$C$33,$B$32:$B$33)`

</details>
