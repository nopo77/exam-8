# 2002 Exam 9 - Q41

**Points:** 2

## Question

Evaluate the appropriateness of the following personal automobile physical damage deductible factors.

| Amount of Deductible | Factor |
| --- | --- |
| Full Coverage | 1.05 |
| $100 | 1.00 |
| $250 | 0.95 |
| $500 | 0.90 |
| $1,000 | 0.75 |
| $1,500 | 0.60 |

## Solution

This was an unusual question, since normally we apply the consistency test to limits, not to deductibles. However, similar principles can apply. First, deductible factors should decrease as the deductible increases. This should be somewhat intuitive - the higher deductible, the less coverage provided, so the insured should get a lower rate. Second, deductible factors should decrease at a decreasing rate as the deductible increases. This is less intuitive, but it is similar to why ILFs should increase at a decreasing rate.

Chg Factor / Chg Ded

-0.00050 — `=(C9-C8)/(B9-0)`

-0.00033 — `=(C10-C9)/(B10-B9)`

-0.00020 — `=(C11-C10)/(B11-B10)`

-0.00030 — `=(C12-C11)/(B12-B11)`

-0.00030 — `=(C13-C12)/(B13-B12)`

The factors are not appropriate since they fail the consistency test. The deductible factors should decrease at a decreasing rate.

0.53 — `=(J12-J11)/(B11-B10)*1000000`

-0.20 — `=(J13-J12)/(B12-B11)*1000000` — <-- remember that decreasing at a decreasing rate means a positive 2nd derivative, so a negative value fails the test.

0.00 — `=(J14-J13)/(B13-B12)*1000000`
