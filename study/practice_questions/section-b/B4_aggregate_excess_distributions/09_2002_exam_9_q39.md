## Solution

Since the question mentions a "retention" instead of a "deductible", I will assume for parts (a) and (b) that this is an excess policy, not a large deductible policy, so the insurer only pays for LAE on excess losses. That way, the Loss Conversion Factor will be multiplied by excess losses.

Solution Approach 1 for (a) and (b): Use vertical slices

### Part a

In this part, there is only the occurrence retention, and no aggregate limit. So the policy covers losses above the retention.

| J | K | L |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loss XS of 250k | $275,000 |  |
| Expected XS Loss | $27,500 | This is equivalent to E[A] - E[A_D] |

<details><summary>Formulas</summary>

- `K10` = `=SUM(C25:C27)-250000*3`
- `K11` = `=K10/10`

</details>

**Price per policy:** $30,250 — `=K11*B8`

### Part b

**E[A]:** $600,000 — `=AVERAGE(F11:F20)`

E[A_D] — $572,500 — `=K15-K10/10` — retained

**L_G:** $858,750 — `=1.5*K17`

From here, the fastest way to solve this is using vertical slices to get the charge. We need to calculate risk 9 and 10 retained losses to see if they exceed L_G.

| J | L | M |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retained for risk 9 | $850,000 | below L_G |
| Retained for risk 10 | $975,000 | above L_G, use to get charge |

<details><summary>Formulas</summary>

- `L24` = `=F19-(C25-250000)`
- `L25` = `=F20-SUM(C26:C27)+2*250000`

</details>

The insurance charge is just the expected retained loss above the aggregate max.

Ins Charge — $11,625 — `=(L25-K19)*(1/10)` — This is equivalent to E[A_D] - E[A_D;L_G]

**Converted charge:** $12,787.50 — `=L29*B8`
