# 2008 Exam 9 - Q33

**Points:** 2

## Question

While reviewing the quotes for several loss sensitive plans, an actuary discovers that the expected losses, on which the excess loss cost estimates are based, are 20% too low.

The actuary has separated the loss sensitive plans into two distinct groups:

- Small risks with per occurrence loss limits of $100,000 and expected limited losses of $500,000, and
- Larger risks with per occurrence loss limits of $1,000,000 and expected limited losses of $3,000,000.

Each plan has an aggregate loss limit of 150% of the initial, incorrect, expected limited losses.

### Part a (1.00 pts)

The underwriter asserts that, because aggregate loss limits are well above the corrected loss estimates, there will be no impact on profitability. Provide an appropriate actuarial response to this assertion.

### Part b (0.50 pts)

Briefly discuss how the impact of the estimation error on aggregate excess loss adequacy varies between smaller and larger sized risks.

### Part c (0.50 pts)

Briefly discuss how the impact of the estimation error on per occurrence excess loss adequacy varies between smaller and larger sized risks.

## Solution

### Part a

If the aggregate loss limits are set too low (based on incorrect expected losses), then there is a higher probability of losses exceeding these limits, and there should be an additional charge for this. Without that higher charge, the pricing for the expected excess losses will be inadequate.

### Part b

The dollar error in charges is greatest for large policies (at low entry ratios). The percentage error in charges is greatest for large policies (at high entry ratios).

### Part c

I assume the estimation error has no impact on the occurrence limits selected or the XS ratios (this would be the case if we looked up the excess ratios, like we do when we use the NCCI manual). In that case, we have:

Expected losses in excess of the per occurrence limit = expected ground-up losses × XS ratio

The per occurrence charges would be 20% too low for all risk sizes if based on expected ground-up losses that were 20% too low (and we assume the XS ratios don't change). In dollar terms this will be larger for larger risks.
